F & Q

Everything you need to know about reBot Arm, open-source resources, and getting started

01

Who is reBot Arm for? Can a beginner get started?

reBot Arm is built for developers, researchers, students, and AI innovators exploring Embodied AI. Beginners can start effortlessly: try our zero-code SenseCraft platform or follow our 19-module Learning Physical AI course (20+ hours) from basic kinematics to Sim-to-Real VLA deployment. Read Beginner Guide on Wiki →

02

What's the difference between reBot Arm DM and RS? Which one should I choose?

The DM edition uses Damiao actuators (1.5 kg rated payload, ±0.2 mm repeatability), ideal for beginners, education, and budget-conscious setups. The RS edition uses RobStride actuators (2.5 kg rated payload, ±0.1 mm repeatability, higher torque), built for VLA research labs and high-frequency data collection. Compare Specs on Wiki →

03

Is reBot Arm fully open-source? What exactly is open?

Yes — reBot Arm is 100% open-source across hardware and software. We provide STEP/CAD files, BOM, motor firmware SDKs, URDF/MJCF digital twins, ROS 2 drivers, and LeRobot training pipelines so you can freely customize and extend the arm. Explore Open-Source Docs on Wiki →

04

What programming languages and frameworks does reBot Arm support?

reBot Arm supports Python, C++, ROS 2 Humble, and Hugging Face LeRobot out of the box. You can run zero-code workflows in SenseCraft, develop kinematics with Pinocchio, or train Sim-to-Real policies in MuJoCo and NVIDIA Isaac Sim. View Framework Guides on Wiki →

05

Which depth cameras are compatible? Do I need to buy one separately?

reBot Arm is officially validated with Orbbec Gemini 2, Orbbec Gemini 336, Intel RealSense D435i, RealSense D405, and standard UVC USB cameras. Cameras are available separately or in station bundles with 3D-printable wrist and top mounts. See Camera Setup on Wiki →

06

What kind of computer do I need? Any GPU requirements?

Any modern laptop handles basic control, teleoperation, and our browser-based MuJoCo simulator. For local VLA/ACT policy training and inference, we recommend an NVIDIA GPU (8GB+ VRAM) or our reComputer J3011 (Jetson Orin Nano 8GB) edge box. Check System Requirements on Wiki →

07

What's included in the Learning Physical AI course? Is it free with the arm?

Co-developed with NVIDIA DLI, the course features 19 hands-on modules across 5 chapters (20+ hours), covering 3D vision, ROS 2, Isaac Sim, and VLA deployment. Core documentation is open on our Wiki, or you can grab the Arm + Course bundle. Browse Full Syllabus on Wiki →

08

Are there tutorials and community support available?

Yes! We maintain step-by-step Wiki documentation for both DM and RS editions, covering calibration, ROS 2, LeRobot, and Isaac Sim. Our engineering team and global community provide active support on GitHub Issues and Discord. Visit Official reBot Wiki →

09

What is sim-to-real? Can I try reBot Arm without buying the hardware first?

Sim-to-Real lets you train and validate control policies in physics simulation before deploying to physical hardware. You can test the interactive reBot digital twin right now in our free browser-based MuJoCo simulator — zero hardware required. Read Simulator Guide on Wiki →

10

What's the shipping and warranty policy?

We ship globally within 3–5 business days (typical delivery in 7–14 days). Every reBot Arm includes a 1-year limited warranty covering manufacturing defects, plus full access to open-source replacement part files and technical support. Check Support Details on Wiki →
